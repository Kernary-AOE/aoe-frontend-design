# FullLandingGallery [example] v1.0.0
20 curated full-page landing screenshots from Land-book Pro, tagged by pattern type and persona lean. Full-page screenshots capture what section-level galleries cannot: page rhythm, section sequencing, visual consistency, and scroll narrative. A hero section and a pricing section may each look good in isolation — but a full-page view reveals whether transitions are smooth, whether visual density varies appropriately across the scroll, and whether the brand aesthetic holds from top to bottom.
domain: frontend-design

## Observations
- Dark-palette products maintain tonal consistency throughout — deviation is rare. Across all dark-theme full pages (aira, harmonicai, intelligent-management-platform, paperclip, stocks-bonds-crypto), the dark background is continuous across all sections with only subtle tonal variation. There are no 'light section islands' inside an otherwise dark page. This is a strong convention: breaking tonal consistency mid-page reads as unfinished rather than varied.
- Editorial and luxury brands use photography as the primary section separator. Aspelin-reitan, bedouins-daughter, goldsand, lucci-lambrusco — in all four, full-bleed photography acts as both content and visual breathing room between copy-heavy sections. The photography is the layout. Copy is overlaid or adjacent, never competing.
- The canonical B2B SaaS scroll sequence is: hero → features → social proof → pricing → CTA. Aira, gogetta, harmonicai, and bright-os all follow this structure. This sequence is so established that departing from it — especially moving pricing above social proof — is a conversion risk unless there is deliberate intent (e.g., transparent pricing as a differentiator).
- Illustrated / mascot brands maintain character across all sections. Gogetta uses its illustrated mascot in the hero, features, and footer. This creates brand coherence that photographic or icon-based brands rarely achieve with the same vividness. It comes at a cost: the illustration system must be extensive enough to populate multiple section types.
- Studio / portfolio pages invert the SaaS hierarchy — work comes before process. Stodio, vogelhaus, and to a degree anima lead with work output (portfolio cases, design demos) before explaining services. The rationale: show craft, then explain context. This is opposite to product SaaS pages where the value proposition must precede the proof.

## Agent Workflow
```
    1. Use full-page samples to define section order, not section content. When planning a landing page architecture, select 2 full-page samples that match the target persona and inspect the section sequence. The section-level galleries (hero, features, pricing, CTA, testimonials) then fill in the detail.
    2. Check tonal consistency mode. Determine early whether the design should be dark-continuous, light-continuous, or mixed-light-with-dark-accents. The full-page gallery demonstrates that successful pages commit to one mode.
    3. Select 1 full-page + 2-3 section samples as an agent's reference set. The full-page anchor establishes rhythm; section samples add pattern detail. This set of 3-4 images is the practical limit for vision-model consumption.
    4. Cross-reference section galleries for each section in the full-page. If a full-page sample's hero section is interesting, look for it (or similar items) in the hero gallery. The section galleries provide closer inspection.
    5. Studio portfolios are not SaaS templates. Stodio, vogelhaus, and anima are high-quality but serve a different purchase decision. Do not apply their section hierarchy to a B2B SaaS product without deliberate adaptation.
  
```
