### 1.1 The undeclared majority is a recoverable rejection, not a permanent one

`1320` units are rejected only because they state no `source.license`.
`../prime-corpus.yaml` sets `requireDeclared: true`, so a set cannot claim them.
That is the declaration working as intended: an unlicensed unit is not
automatically the corpus owner's to publish. Most of these are plausibly
original work by this project's own contributors — but "plausibly" is not a
licence, and the fix is to establish provenance per unit, not to relax the flag.

### 1.2 The copyleft rejections are the load-bearing ones

`94` units declare GPL-3.0. `../prime-corpus.yaml` already predicts this
case in a comment on `license.policy.deny`: copyleft that cannot be
redistributed under Apache-2.0 "must fail the gate if a later migration pulls it
in, rather than arriving unnoticed". Taking a GPL-3.0 unit into an Apache-2.0
corpus would put the whole distribution under GPL-3.0's reciprocal obligation —
which is why the answer is rejection and not attribution.

### 1.3 `public leak` is the category with no repair path

`160` units under `@M14` carry the literal licence string `public leak`.
`primes/modules/M14-ai-prompts/MANIFEST.md` describes the module as extracted
from "leaked and public system prompts of major AI coding tools". There is no
grant to receive: the upstream never licensed the material, and republication
would redistribute someone else's proprietary prompt. No attribution, notice or
policy change makes this admissible. These stay out permanently.

## 2. What rejection does not mean

Rejection is scoped to *this corpus package's* redistribution. It does not mean
the units are wrong, stale or deletable. They remain in `primes/` under the
engine repo, where they are not published. Deleting them is a separate decision
that this lane did not make and has no evidence for.
